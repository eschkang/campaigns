## AmeriSat Global Plug-and-Play Deployment Brief

**Summary**  
AmeriSat Global shifts to a turnkey, Starlink-style model for partner governments, UN implementers, and other international institutions. We drop-ship a ready-to-go terminal, they flip it on, and everyone at the school, clinic, or civic campus connects over Wi-Fi. Billing is handled through the host ministry via a monthly stewardship invoice—the rest of the complexity stays inside the KNOC and AmeriSat Global operations.

**Key Elements**
1. **Hardware-as-a-service** – Each terminal arrives preconfigured with the satellite modem, Wi-Fi access point, and solar/battery kit. Installation is plug-and-play; no portals or additional registration are required for end users.
2. **One-click experience** – Telemedicine, education, and civic traffic is prioritized by KNOC policies so the moment the terminal is online, users can “press video/telemed” and expect a fast connection.
3. **Ministry billing** – The partner ministry or implementing agency receives a simple stewardship invoice covering monitoring, support, and data capacity. Payments replenish the AmeriSat Global operating pool, covering spare parts, technicians, and next-terminal deployments.
4. **Light operational oversight** – Instead of requiring every facility to master ASSAA, we train a small ministry operations desk to read the usage summary and confirm SLAs; KNOC sends automated status digests, and the ministry responds with a single acknowledgement email.
5. **Resilient operations center** – KNOC still manages firmware updates, priority routing, and rapid response. If a terminal falls below the SLA, AmeriSat Global dispatches a technician under the local support program without requiring the facility to log into a portal.

**UN GA messaging angles**
- Emphasize that any country can get connectivity instantly: drop-ship the terminal, flip the switch, share the Wi-Fi, and AmeriSat Global handles the rest.  
- Highlight that the operation is accountable without burdensome bureaucracy—the ministry simply receives a monthly invoice and a status memo.  
- Point to the U.S. program as the same engine powering the global service, proving that a public-benefit satellite fleet can deliver plug-and-play access everywhere.
